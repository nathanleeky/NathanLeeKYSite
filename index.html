A.I. Philosophy

How I think about AI solving real selling challenges.

AI automates the administrative, not the relational

The best use of AI in sales is automating the work that keeps salespeople from selling. Not the prospecting call, the CRM data entry. Not the negotiation, the contract routing. Not the close, the deal documentation. We're using the wrong tools to solve sales problems if we're automating the human skills that actually create value.

Enablement is about eliminating friction

Every tool, process, and framework should have a clear answer to: "What problem does this solve for the person using it?" If the answer is "it helps the company measure things" or "it gives leaders visibility," you've built something that salespeople will work around. Real enablement is about making their job easier.

Systems scale consistency, not shortcuts

Bad systems are worse than no systems. Good systems are invisible. They make it easier to do the right thing than to cut corners. They distribute expertise so new team members can do what experienced ones do. They document lessons learned so patterns improve over time.

Adoption requires trust

People adopt tools, processes, and strategies when they trust that (1) leadership believes in it, (2) it will actually help them, and (3) they were part of shaping it. Building adoption means real listening, real incorporation of feedback, and real transparency about why things work the way they do.
