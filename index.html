The problem

Our top relationship managers were running client deepening strategy sessions once a year, mostly off memory and scattered spreadsheets. Good RMs know their clients. But knowing isn't the same as having the data in front of you when you need it.

What I owned

I owned the strategy and rollout for our client deepening program, where RMs take their top 8-12 clients each year, sit down with product partners, and pitch new solutions. That meant designing the annual cadence, driving change management across RMs and product partners, and building the reporting underneath it, including a unified intelligence platform on Power BI and Tableau pulling from multiple Salesforce instances, covering 500+ users and 12,000+ accounts.

The result

Average deepening revenue per client roughly doubled year over year, from around $60k to $130k+ on average, program-wide.

The problem

Ahead of an internal strategy session, the RM walks in already knowing the client cold. The product partners in the room usually don't. Not because they don't care, but because there's no real channel for that information to reach them unless the RM briefs each one by hand.

What I built

An AI-generated packet that goes out by email ten days before the internal strategy session. It pulls from Salesforce, internal source systems, and external data like Dun & Bradstreet, turning all of it into one summary everyone reads before they're in the room.

The result

Manual research time cut by more than half, and product partners walk into the session already caught up instead of hearing the client's story for the first time live.

The problem

Coverage existed, but nobody could see all of it at once. Different groups managed their own account and territory assignment models—some by geography, some by industry, some by account size—with no shared documentation. If you needed to know who owned a given account or prospect, the honest answer was usually "check a spreadsheet, or ask around."

What I built

I led the initiative to fix that. I benchmarked how competing banks handle territory coverage, pulled in research from firms like McKinsey, and turned that into a concrete recommendation: a governed data source with defined owners for each model, documentation standards every group would follow, and a searchable coverage directory that answers who covers what and why. I didn't stop at the recommendation. I built a working prototype of the lookup tool myself, so leadership could see the concept before being asked to invest in it.

The result

Leadership signed off on the governance model and phased roadmap. This one's still active—part of the point: this is the kind of ambiguous, cross-team problem that lands on my desk, and how I go about solving it.

AI Philosophy

I don't just implement AI tools. I think carefully about when and how they actually work.

AI works best when invisible and just works. The moment your team thinks about the AI, it's too visible. Good AI solutions disappear into the workflow.

AI can't solve broken or incomplete workflows. If your process is already messy, adding AI just automates the mess. Fix the workflow first, then add intelligence.

Match complexity to capability. Don't design an AI solution at complexity level 9 if your team is at fluency level 4. Meet people where they are.

The real work is adoption. Building the AI is the easy part. Getting sales teams to actually use it, trust it, and embed it into their day is the hard part.
